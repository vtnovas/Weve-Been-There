Prepare For Your Exam What to Expect

It is time for your medical exam! But how do you set one up, and what do you need to bring with you? Here, you will find everything you need to know before you set foot in the examination room.

Who to Schedule Your Exam With

Ultimately, this depends on what pathway you are on.

Adjustment of Status

You will need to schedule an exam with an approved USCIS Civil Surgeon. If you are working with an attorney, usually they will have a list of contacts for you to choose from. If you are not, you can use the below USCIS tool to find one near you.

Find a Civil Surgeon

Consular Processing

When you get the notice from the NVC that your interview has been scheduled, that is your cue to schedule an exam. The interview notice and/or the embassy's immigrant visa instructions will direct you to the approved panel physicians you can choose from.

What You Need to Bring

Again, this depends on your pathway. You can find a checklist for both below.

Adjustment of Status

  • Government-issued Photo ID
  • Vaccine Records
  • Medical Records*
  • List of Medications*
  • Payment

Consular Processing

  • Passport
  • Interview Appointment Letter or NVC Interview Notice
  • DS-260 Confirmation Page
  • Vaccine Records
  • Medical Records*
  • List of Medications*
  • Passport-style Photos*
  • Payment

*if it applies to you

Vaccination Requirements

If you have not received the necessary vaccines, the civil surgeon or panel physician (depending on your pathway) may give them to you or send you to get them yourself and then return.

Nervous for your appointment, or just like to be informed? Here is a step-by-step walkthrough of what you can expect to happen during your appointment.

  1. 1

    Check-in

    The clinic reviews your ID, appointment information, vaccination records, and payment.

  2. 2

    Medical history review

    The doctor or staff asks about prior illnesses, surgeries, medications, vaccines, mental health history, substance use, and any past TB treatment.

  3. 3

    Physical exam

    The doctor performs a basic exam, such as checking height, weight, blood pressure, eyes, ears, throat, heart, lungs, abdomen, and general physical condition.

  4. 4

    Required testing

    Depending on age and immigration category, this may include:

    • Tuberculosis testing or chest X-ray
    • Blood test for syphilis
    • Gonorrhea testing
    • Other testing if medically indicated
  5. 5

    Vaccination review

    The doctor checks whether you have the required vaccines. If records are missing, they may give vaccines, order blood tests to prove immunity, or mark certain vaccines as not medically appropriate when allowed.

  6. 6

    Additional follow-up, if needed

    Some applicants may need extra testing, treatment, or specialist records before the exam can be completed.

  7. 7

    Results are completed

    For adjustment of status, the civil surgeon completes Form I-693 and usually gives it to you in a sealed envelope for USCIS.

    For consular processing, the panel physician usually sends results directly to the consulate or gives you sealed results, depending on that country's process.

DO NOT OPEN THE SEALED RESULTS

If you receive a sealed envelope, keep it sealed. USCIS or the consulate will likely reject it if it has been opened.
