What Are They? What Can I Do With Them

Many people applying for a family-based green card through adjustment of status also apply for a work permit (also called employment authorization) and a Social Security number. These can make everyday life in the United States much easier while your green card application is still being processed.

Employment Authorization Document (EAD)

An EAD, or also called a work permit, is a card issued by USCIS that allows you to legally work in the United States while your green card application is pending.

If you are applying using the adjustment of status pathway from within the United States, you may be eligible to apply one of these by filing Form I-765, Application for Employment Authorization. Many applicants file this form at the same time as their green card application.

Once it is approved, USCIS will mail you your work permit.

Social Security Number (SSN)

A Social Security number is a unique number issued by the Social Security Administration (SSA). It is used to report wages, pay taxes, and verify your identity for employment and other everyday activities.

Many adjustment of status applicants can request a Social Security number as part of their application for an EAD. If eligible, the Social Security Administration will typically mail your Social Security card after your work permit is approved.

A Social Security number is not an immigration status or a work permit. It is an identification number that you will use throughout your life in the United States.

Protect Your Social Security Number

Your Social Security number (SSN) is sensitive personal information. Only share it when it is truly needed, such as for a job, taxes, or opening a bank account. Keep your Social Security card in a safe place at home, and be cautious of anyone who asks for your SSN. Protecting your SSN can help prevent identity theft and fraud.
