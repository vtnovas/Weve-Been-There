Know Your Type

Receiving your green card is an exciting milestone! Once your application is approved, you will become a lawful permanent resident (LPR) of the United States.

Most family-based applicants receive a 10-year green card. However, if you are receiving your green card through marriage and have been married for less than two years when you become a permanent resident, you will receive a 2-year conditional green card instead.

10-Year

Most family-based green card holders receive a 10-year green card. This card proves that you are a lawful permanent resident. While the card itself expires every 10 years, your permanent resident status does not. To keep your proof of status up to date, you will generally need to renew your card before it expires by filing Form I-90, Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card.

2-Year Conditional

If you are receiving your green card through marriage and you have been married for less than two years on the day you become a lawful permanent resident, you will receive a 2-year conditional green card.

A conditional green card gives you the same rights to live and work in the United States as a person with a 10-year green card. The difference is that USCIS requires one additional step before issuing a regular 10-year card, which you will do 90 days before this one expires.

Your Rights and Responsibilities

Becoming a lawful permanent resident (green card holder) gives you the right to live and work in the United States permanently. It also comes with important responsibilities. Understanding both can help you protect your status.

Your Rights Your Responsibilities Protect Your Status

Your Rights

As a green card holder, you can generally:

  • Live permanently in the United States, as long as you do not commit actions that could make you removable under immigration law.
  • Work in the United States if you are qualified for the job.
  • Be protected by all federal, state, and local laws.
  • Own property and manage your finances.
  • Attend public schools and colleges.
  • Apply for U.S. citizenship if you become eligible.

Your Responsibilities

As a green card holder, you are expected to:

  • Obey all federal, state, and local laws.
  • File federal, state, and local income tax returns when required.
  • Register with the Selective Service if you are a male between the ages of 18 and 25 and are required to do so.
  • Carry proof of your permanent resident status, as required by law.
  • Notify USCIS if you move by updating your address within 10 days.

Protect Your Status

Your green card allows you to live permanently in the United States, but it is important to maintain your status.

For example, you should:

  • Avoid abandoning your permanent residence by living outside the United States for long periods without proper planning.
  • Follow U.S. laws and avoid criminal activity that could affect your immigration status.
  • Never claim to be a U.S. citizen if you are not one.
  • Renew your green card before it expires, or file to remove conditions if you have a 2-year conditional green card.

If you have questions about extended travel, criminal charges, or anything else that could affect your permanent resident status, it is a good idea to learn about the rules before taking action.

What's Next?

Receiving your green card is a major milestone, but for many people, it is not the last step in their immigration journey.

What comes next depends on the type of green card you received.

If You Have a 10-Year Green Card ▼

If you have a 10-year green card, you will simply renew your green card when it expires by filing Form I-90, Application to Replace Permanent Resident Card.

Remember, your permanent resident status does not expire when your green card expires. However, it is important to renew your green card so you always have valid proof of your status.

If You Have a 2-Year Conditional Green Card ▼

If you have a 2-year conditional green card, you will need to file Form I-751, Petition to Remove Conditions on Residence, before your card expires. File the petition together during the 90-day period before your conditional green card expires.

If USCIS approves your petition, you will receive a 10-year green card.

Field Notes

Don't Miss This Deadline

Filing Form I-751 on time is an important step in maintaining your permanent resident status.

When Can I Apply for Citizenship?

Many green card holders eventually choose to become U.S. citizens through a process called naturalization.

In general:

  • If you are married to and living with a U.S. citizen, you may be eligible to apply for citizenship after 3 years as a lawful permanent resident, if you meet all other eligibility requirements.
  • Most other lawful permanent residents may be eligible after 5 years as a lawful permanent resident.

Meeting the time requirement does not automatically make you eligible. You must also meet other requirements, such as continuous residence, physical presence, good moral character, and English and civics requirements (unless an exception applies).
