What Is It?

A biometrics appointment is one of the first steps many applicants complete after filing their green card application. During this appointment, USCIS collects information that helps confirm your identity and conduct background and security checks. In most cases, this includes:

Fingerprints

A Photograph

Your Signature

Do All Applicants Need Biometrics?

Many family-based green card applicants are required to attend a biometrics appointment, although USCIS may sometimes reuse biometrics that were previously collected for another immigration application.

The Appointment Notice

If USCIS needs your biometrics, they will send you an appointment notice by mail. This notice will tell you:

  • The date of your appointment
  • The time of your appointment
  • The address of the Application Support Center (ASC)
  • What documents you should bring

Read the Notice Carefully

When you receive your appointment notice, take a moment to review all the information. Make sure:

  • The appointment date and time work for you
  • You know how to get to the appointment location
  • You understand what documents you need to bring

If you believe you cannot attend the appointment, follow the instructions from USCIS to reschedule as soon as possible.

Biometrics Information
Field Notes

You May Need to Travel

Note that the location may not be in the city or state you are currently in. Please make sure to check the information carefully so you can make travel plans if needed.

Keep the Notice in a Safe Place

You will typically need to bring the original appointment notice with you to the biometrics appointment.

What If the Notice Never Arrives?

Sometimes applicants see an update in their USCIS online account saying that a biometrics appointment was scheduled, but the notice never arrives in the mail.

If this happens, you may be able to access a copy of the notice through your USCIS online account. If not, you may need to contact USCIS for assistance.

What to Expect

For most applicants, the biometrics appointment is one of the easiest steps in the green card process. Many people are in and out within 15 to 30 minutes, but wait times can vary depending on how busy the office is.

Before You Go

Make sure to bring:

  • Your biometrics appointment notice
  • A valid government-issued photo ID (such as a passport, driver's license, or state ID)

USCIS will list any additional requirements on your appointment notice.

The Appointment

It is a good idea to arrive a little early in case there is a line or security screening at the entrance. After checking in, a USCIS staff member will:

1

Verify Your Identity

2

Take Your Fingerprints

3

Take a Photograph

4

Collect Your Signature

The process is quick and usually does not involve questions about your green card application.

After the Appointment

After the appointment is complete, the next step is often another waiting period while USCIS continues reviewing the case. USCIS will use your biometrics to complete required background and security checks as your case continues moving through the process.
