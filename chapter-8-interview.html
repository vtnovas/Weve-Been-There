Who Gets One?

Not everyone applying for a family-based green card will have an interview, but many applicants do.

Whether you will have an interview depends on your type of application and how USCIS reviews your case. If an interview is required, USCIS will send you an appointment notice with the date, time, and location.

Adjustment of Status Applicants

If you are applying using the adjustment of status pathway, USCIS may schedule an interview at your local field office.

Many family-based adjustment of status applicants are interviewed, especially spouses of U.S. citizens and lawful permanent residents. However, USCIS has the authority to waive the interview for some applicants if they determine it is not necessary.

Consular Processing Applicants

If you are applying for your immigrant visa from outside the United States, you should expect to attend an interview at a U.S. embassy or consulate in your home country or country of residence. This interview is an important step before an immigrant visa can be issued.

How Will I Know?

If an interview is required, USCIS or the National Visa Center (NVC) will notify you by mail or through your online account. Your appointment notice will include instructions about when to appear and what documents to bring.

Until you receive an interview notice, there is nothing you need to do except continue checking your mail and your online account for updates.

How to Prepare

It is normal to feel nervous before your interview. The good news is that you can prepare ahead of time. The interview is your chance to confirm the information in your application and answer any questions the immigration officer may have.

Practice Together

If your case is based on a marriage, spend some time reviewing your application together before the interview. Focus on making sure you both remember important details and tell your story accurately.

You can practice talking about:

  • How you met
  • Your first dates
  • When your relationship became serious
  • Your engagement or wedding
  • Where you live together
  • Your daily routines
  • Important dates, such as birthdays or anniversaries
  • Future plans as a couple

If you notice that one of you remembers a detail differently, talk about it before the interview. Reviewing your timeline together can help you feel more confident.

Field Notes

Don't Try to Memorize Answers

Officers are looking for honest answers. It's okay if you don't remember every small detail. If you don't know or can't remember something, it's better to say so than to guess.

Gather Your Documents

Your interview notice will tell you what you need, but generally, you should bring:

  • Your interview appointment notice
  • A valid government-issued photo ID (such as a passport)
  • Original civil documents, if requested
  • Updated financial documents, if requested
  • Evidence supporting your application, if requested
  • Any additional documents listed in your appointment notice

If your case is based on marriage, it is also a good idea to bring updated evidence showing that your relationship has continued since you filed your application. Examples include:

  • Recent photos together
  • Joint bank account statements
  • Lease or mortgage documents
  • Utility bills
  • Insurance policies
  • Birth certificates of children you share
Field Notes

Review Your Application

Before your interview, read through the forms and documents you submitted to USCIS or the National Visa Center. Be prepared to answer questions about the information you provided.

If anything has changed since you filed your application, such as your address, job, or family situation, be prepared to let the officer know.

Field Notes

Get Ready for the Day

Plan to arrive early, dress neatly, and give yourself extra time for parking and security screening. Bringing an organized folder with your documents can make the check-in process much easier and help you feel more prepared.

What to Expect

Every interview is different, but most follow a similar process. The officer will review your application, ask questions about your case, and confirm that you are eligible for a green card.

When You Arrive During the Interview

When You Arrive

Plan to arrive a little early. After checking in, you may need to go through a security screening before entering the waiting area.

During the Interview

Most family-based green card interviews last between 20 and 45 minutes, although some may be shorter or longer depending on the case.

During your interview, the officer will usually:

  • Verify your identity
  • Place you under oath to tell the truth
  • Review your application
  • Ask questions about your eligibility
  • Request any additional documents, if needed
If Your Case Is Based on Marriage ▼

You and your spouse will usually attend the interview together. In most cases, you will be interviewed together in the same room. The officer will most likely ask questions about your relationship to better understand your history together. These questions help the officer confirm that your marriage is genuine.

If the officer has concerns or needs more information, they may decide to interview each of you separately. This is sometimes called a Stokes interview. It does not happen in most cases, but it is a tool USCIS may use to better understand a relationship if questions arise. Most applicants however, complete a standard interview together and do not have a separate interview.
